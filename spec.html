Design & results

What the tool does, in plain English — and the real numbers it produces.

Engine complete · 119/119 green in Octave + MATLAB

The scenario

An interceptor missile flies head-on at a cruising drone; their paths pass about 5 m apart. As they draw level, the warhead detonates (after a tiny electronics delay). Hundreds of metal fragments blast outward at 800–1200 m/s and are also carried forward at the interceptor's speed. Over the next few milliseconds the tool follows each fragment and works out which strike the drone, where, when, and with how much energy.

What it computes

  • Closing speed — head-on, the interceptor and drone speeds add: 226.4 m/s.
  • Fragments — a synthetic placeholder set now; a real fragmentation-data file drops in later.
  • Hit detection — each fragment's flight is traced and tested against the drone's 3-D surface (which part, where, when).
  • Kinetic energy — the damage-relevant energy at impact, using the closing speed.
  • Drag & gravity — optional; drag alone cuts impact energy ~20% over the 5 m, so the tool reports a no-drag upper bound and the realistic with-drag figure.
  • Timing & the fire-early lead — how much sooner to fire so the fragment pattern lands on the drone.
  • Coverage & density — how much of the drone is struck, and how concentrated the hits are.

The real output

This is exactly what the finished tool prints (default synthetic fragments, drag off):

  Electronics delay: target displacement 0.061-0.105 m  vs  along-track closure 0.158-0.272 m  (~2.6x)
  Forward offset: fragment-carry 1.216 m + electronics-closure 0.158-0.272 m
  Fire-early lead 1.374-1.487 m  (6.069-6.569 ms) | ideal: centroid on drone
Why “wing 0”. The drone is a crude placeholder shape (flat plates, swappable). Its thin wing sits edge-on and is shadowed by the body, and fragments drift ~1.2 m rearward, so hits land on the body & tail. This is a placeholder-shape effect, not a modelling error — the hit-detection is independently validated. A real drone STL (from the mentor) gives the true wing split.

Where it runs

The physics core runs unmodified in both GNU Octave 11.3 and MATLAB R2022b — 119/119 validation checks pass in both. The 3-D animation is MATLAB (bundle 4, next); this hub is the web review surface (bundle 5). Every number is sourced from the mentor's proven fragment-dispersion physics.
