The diagonal-burst idea

Front view — the drone is flying straight at you. Left–right = the 12 m wingspan. Up–down = height. The dashed ring is every point that's 5 m from the target.

The 5 m miss distance is radial — the interceptor can pass 5 m away in any direction, and the airburst is 360°. The drone is a thin, wide plate. So the burst is only a problem in the narrow red zones (level with the wings, straight out to the side). Everywhere else — the whole green ring — the burst clears the wings and the drone sits cleanly on one side.

Put the burst on a diagonal (green zone) → wings clear, one clean side, no model change

The takeaway: your idea is geometrically correct. Keep the 5 m miss distance, but place the burst off the wing plane (a realistic diagonal or over-the-top pass). The whole 6 m-wing drone then sits on one side of the burst, so the validated one-sided physics holds unchanged — and it's arguably a more realistic intercept than a dead-level side-swipe.
