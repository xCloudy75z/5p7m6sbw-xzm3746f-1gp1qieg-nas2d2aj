Measuring the 5 m from the skin fixes the side-on case

Front view — drone flying at you, wings spread left–right. This is the dead-level 3 o'clock intercept that "couldn't" be done.

A miss distance is measured from the skin — the nearest part of the aircraft — not the centreline. So a level side-on pass sits 5 m beyond the wingtip, which puts the burst outside the whole drone. Every part of the drone is then on one clean side, and the validated physics holds — even straight out to the side.
